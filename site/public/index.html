The teleprompter that listens.

Podstudio is a podcast studio that runs in your browser. The script scrolls as you speak, and it records lossless WAV with every retake, cough and ad-lib marked, on your own server.

Follow along on GitHub What works today

Audio only · Chrome, Edge and Arc on computers · iPhone and iPad with iOS 17+ · Android

What it does

01

A prompter that follows you

Voice follow scrolls the script as you talk, and holds your place when you go off script.

02

Lossless recording

Mono or stereo WAV at 16 or 24-bit, saved every 5 s and sent to your server as you record.

03

Marked, not cut

Retake, cough and ad-lib are one key each. The raw file stays whole; the export adds an edit with your last take of each line.

04

A guest and a producer

A 6-digit code and a waiting room. Your guest records on their own device, kept in sync with yours, while you talk on your usual call.

05

Hotkey pads

Nine sounds on the number keys, ducked under your voice and saved as their own track.

06

Yours to run

One small server with two-factor sign-in, a guided installer, and no subscription. MIT licensed.

Questions

If the recordings drift out of sync, can they be lined up again?

Yes, like cameras with timecode. Everyone in a session shares one clock, and every track notes where it is on that clock every 5 seconds. At export Podstudio uses those points to correct the drift between devices and to fill any gap where a guest's audio dropped out, so the tracks stay lined up to the end. Every WAV also carries its start as Broadcast WAV timecode, so your editor can line the files up on its own.

Which editors can use the timecode?

Most professional ones can place a file at its Broadcast WAV timestamp, including Pro Tools, Reaper, Logic Pro, Cubase and Nuendo, DaVinci Resolve and Premiere Pro. You don't need it for a normal export: every file starts at the same moment and runs the same length, so dropping them all at the start of the timeline lines them up in any editor, Audacity and GarageBand included. Timecode helps when you trim or move files first, or bring in audio from a camera or another recorder.

Is Podstudio a call app like Zoom?

No. It records; it doesn't carry audio between people. Talk on Zoom, Teams, Discord or a phone call while each person's device records their own mic, with headphones on so the call doesn't leak in.

Does it need the internet?

Recording works without it, and saves every 5 seconds. Voice follow uses the browser's speech recognition, which in Chrome sends audio to Google to turn it into text, so the device doing voice follow needs a connection. Guests upload to your server as they record.

What does it cost?

Nothing: it's free and MIT licensed, with no paid tier. You run it on your own server, like a small VPS, which is where your recordings live.
